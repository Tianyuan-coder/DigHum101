The lockdown of COVID-19 changed our lives and experiences as students. Through Kaggle, we found the dataset “Reddit messages from selected universities,” based on the research article titled, "Has Sentiment Returned to the Pre-pandemic Level? A Sentiment Analysis Using College Subreddit Data from 2019 to 2022 in the U.S."  It was released under MIT license and stated that permission is granted to use the data without restriction. Our interest in sentiment analysis and involvement in this time period brought up a few questions for us such as: How did student sentiment shift across time? What types of emotions or topics were most prevalent, and how did their prominence change? To what extent did regional, climatic, institutional factors, or current events correspond with sentiment differences across campuses? Though limiting factors prevented us from including outside data and searching for all of these answers, we decided to follow the way that the dataset grouped universities (west, midwest, south, and east) by choosing a university from each region to analyze their individual sentiments and compare them, and aimed to answer the question, “What do the shifts in sentiment look like for each region over this time period and how do they compare with one another?” As Berkeley students, we wondered more specifically how the other universities would compare with ours. We wanted to explore the emotions embodied in these students' posts and whether these patterns might be related.
This dataset contains csvs of Reddit post content and institutional attributes (e.g., school name, region, selectivity) for 128 U.S. universities for each year (2019, 202, 2021, 2022). In our analysis, we selected 4 schools to focus on: Cornell (east coast), UC Berkeley (west coast), University of Texas at Austin (south), and Michigan State University (midwest). By selecting these schools with a certain geographic representation, we hoped to obtain an implicit meaning or relationship between emotional expressions and region, if not societal events.
